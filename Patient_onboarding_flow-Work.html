1. Context & Problem Statement

In medical and physiotherapy intake workflows, patients often encounter high friction when asked to enter complex insurance policy information before establishing basic trust[cite: 7, 8].

Initial analytical drop-off data showed a 42% abandonment rate when insurance fields appeared prematurely in the onboarding sequence[cite: 7].

Core UX Objective

Re-architect the patient onboarding sequence into a logical, progressive disclosure format (Basic Details → Insurance Details → Injury Details)[cite: 7, 8]. Standardize all UI cards, input controls, and action elements into a cohesive design system[cite: 8].

2. Target Audience & Key Insights

First-Time Patients

Require quick appointment scheduling with minimal initial fields. Expect clear progress indicators[cite: 7, 8].

Family / Dependent Caregivers

Frequently fill forms on behalf of relatives. Need intuitive radio toggles ("Self" vs "Relative")[cite: 12] and easy file uploads[cite: 8, 12].

Clinic Administrators

Rely on structured subscriber identity records and clear card document uploads to eliminate manual billing follow-ups[cite: 8].

3. Task Flow & Architecture

To streamline completion, the flow separates demographic acquisition from financial verification and clinical symptom collection[cite: 7, 8]:

1
Step 1: Basic Details

Collect core patient name, contact info, and preferred doctor selection[cite: 7].

2
Step 2: Insurance Details (Inserted Flow)

Capture insurer relationship, provider selection, member ID, subscriber DOB, and document drag-and-drop[cite: 8, 12].

3
Step 3: Injury Details

Record pain locations, symptom descriptions, and incident history[cite: 7].
